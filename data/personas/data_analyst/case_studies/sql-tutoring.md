# SQL tutoring for a career changer

When I first heard from my client, a small education provider, they were looking for a tutor to help a career‑changer transition into data work. The learner had some experience with spreadsheets but had never written a line of SQL, and they needed to be interview‑ready within a few weeks. I set up a schedule of weekly, two‑hour sessions and focused on the fundamentals – SELECT statements, filtering, joins and basic aggregation – using real‑world examples that mirrored the type of data they would encounter in a tech interview.

Each session began with a brief recap of the previous lesson, followed by a hands‑on exercise. I used PostgreSQL on a local machine, but I only mentioned the tool once to keep the narrative simple. By encouraging the learner to write queries from scratch and then iteratively improve them, they quickly gained confidence. I also introduced best practices for reading error messages and debugging, which proved invaluable when they started tackling practice interview questions.

Beyond the technical bits, I made sure to explain the logic behind each query in plain terms, avoiding jargon. This helped the learner articulate their thought process clearly – a crucial skill when interviewers probe not just the answer but the reasoning. We also covered how to discuss performance considerations without sounding overly technical, which suited their non‑technical background.

The final week we ran through mock interview scenarios, timing each question and providing constructive feedback. When the real interviews arrived, the learner was calm, clear and able to write correct SQL on the spot. They passed both technical interviews and were offered a position as a junior data analyst, marking a successful career change and a rewarding outcome for both of us.
