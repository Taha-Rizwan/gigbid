# Transcribing interviews for a podcast

When a small media start‑up approached me in July 2022, they were looking for someone to turn a series of interview recordings into written transcripts. My usual work revolves around dashboards, SQL pipelines and analytics engineering, so this was a bit of a side‑step – more of a favour for a friend than a typical data‑driven project. Nevertheless, I was keen to help, and I enjoyed the change of pace.

The recordings were hour‑long conversations with a range of guests, from industry experts to everyday storytellers. I set up a simple workflow using the transcription feature in Audacity, which let me listen and type in the same window, pausing and rewinding with a single keystroke. I kept the formatting plain, adding timestamps at the start of each new speaker’s turn so the client could easily locate sections later.

Accuracy was important, especially because the podcast often discussed technical topics. I double‑checked any jargon against the show notes and, where necessary, asked the client for clarification. This extra attention meant the final transcripts were not just verbatim records but also readable documents that captured the nuance of each interview.

By the end of the month I had delivered twelve complete transcripts, each neatly organised in a shared Google Drive folder. The client was delighted; they could now post the text alongside the audio, improve SEO for their website, and even pull quotes for social media without replaying the episodes. It was a satisfying one‑off project that reminded me how valuable clear, well‑structured text can be, even outside the world of dashboards and data pipelines.
