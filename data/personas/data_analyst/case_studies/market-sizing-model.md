# Market sizing spreadsheet for a start-up pitch

When the founders of a fledgling health‑tech start‑up approached me in August 2024, they needed a clear picture of the market they were entering to convince early investors. They had a great product idea but no reliable data on how large the opportunity truly was. I sat down with them over a couple of video calls, listening carefully to their value proposition and the specific customer segments they aimed to serve.

I began by mapping out the value chain and identifying the primary revenue streams they could capture. Using publicly available industry reports and a few targeted surveys, I gathered the necessary assumptions – number of potential users, average spend per user, and adoption rates. I then built a simple, transparent spreadsheet that walked the investors through each step of the calculation, from the top‑down macro figures down to the bottom‑up estimate of reachable customers.

To keep the model easy to update, I linked the key inputs to a single dashboard tab, allowing the founders to tweak assumptions and instantly see the impact on the total addressable market. I also added a small visual chart using Excel’s built‑in tools, so the numbers could be presented at a glance in their pitch deck. Throughout the process I explained the rationale behind each assumption in plain language, ensuring the team felt confident defending the figures.

When the seed deck was completed, the TAM figure from my spreadsheet featured prominently on the slide titled “Market Opportunity”. The investors appreciated the clarity and the realistic grounding of the number, and the start‑up went on to close its seed round shortly thereafter. The client later told me the model had become a living document, regularly refreshed as they gathered more data and expanded into new regions.
